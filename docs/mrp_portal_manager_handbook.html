MRP Portal: Manager's Complete Project Handbook

Audience: managers, product owners, planners, procurement leaders, and business reviewers who are new to MRP and this portal.

Purpose: explain why the portal exists, how a planning run works, what every important screen and label means, what information is required, and what the current implementation does and does not do.

Scope and accuracy note
This handbook describes the project as it is currently implemented in this repository. The portal has a real database-backed CSV import, forward MRP calculation, dashboard, and saved material requirement results. Several surrounding screens are still UI demonstrations with sample/local data and do not yet represent a connected production workflow. Those differences are identified explicitly. This guide is intended to give a complete understanding of the current project, not to imply that every feature shown on screen is production-ready.

1. Executive overview

The MRP Portal helps a planner turn a finished-product forecast into a time-phased plan for the materials needed to make those products. It answers three practical questions:

  1. What materials will the forecast consume, and when?
  2. After existing stock and expected inbound receipts are considered, where will stock fall below its minimum?
  3. What quantity should be planned, and when does its order need to be released based on lead time?

The main planning result is a recommendation. It supports decisions by planning and procurement teams; it does not automatically place an order with a supplier.

In one sentence

Forecast + product recipe (BOM) → material demand → net against stock and receipts → recommend replenishment → review dates, quantities, and risks.

2. MRP in plain language

MRP means Material Requirements Planning. A factory sells or plans finished products, but it must obtain or make the ingredients, packaging, and intermediate items needed to produce them. MRP translates product demand into a dated supply plan for those materials.

A simple example

Suppose a plant plans to make 100 tonnes of a product. Each tonne uses 0.75 tonnes of clinker, and the configured clinker loss/scrap is 1%.


Clinker demand = 100 × 0.75 × (1 + 0.01) = 75.75 tonnes

If the plant starts with 60 tonnes, expects 10 tonnes to arrive, and must retain a 5-tonne minimum, the pre-order stock would be 60 + 10 − 75.75 = −5.75. To finish at the 5-tonne minimum, the net need is 10.75 tonnes. If the minimum order quantity (MOQ) is 12 tonnes, the plan recommends 12 tonnes. The planner then checks whether the release date is early enough for the supplier lead time.

That is the core planning idea. The portal repeats it across plants, materials, and periods, carrying each period's projected stock into the next period.

3. Why this portal was created

The project provides a browser-based way to:

  • bring planning inputs into a shared data model using CSV files;
  • keep plant, product, material, and BOM codes connected;
  • calculate future material needs from forecast and BOM usage;
  • account for opening inventory, scheduled/in-transit supply, safety stock, MOQ, and lead time;
  • show the resulting stock and suggested orders by period;
  • identify orders whose release date is immediately due and stock projections that exceed configured capacity;
  • retain completed calculation runs and inspect material requirements generated by the latest run.

The portal is intended to make planning inputs and recommendations easier to review. The quality of its output depends on the quality of the approved forecast, BOM, units, inventory, receipts, and planning parameters supplied to it.

7. Data needed by the working MRP calculation

The core calculation requires a consistent set of database records:

  1. Country and plant: establishes planning scope and currency.
  2. Material master: defines material description, category, UOM, cost, scrap, lead time, MOQ, order multiple, default safety minimum, and capacity.
  3. Finished-good master: identifies active products at each plant, their type (Bulk or Bagged), and UOM.
  4. BOM: defines component material quantities per finished good. A make material can itself have a component BOM for multilevel explosion.
  5. Demand forecast: expected quantity for each plant/product/bucket date.
  6. Opening inventory: on-hand balance, near-term scheduled receipts, reserved quantity, dated in-transit quantity, and optional stock/capacity overrides.
  7. Plant-material constraints: optional site-specific minimum, reorder reference, max stock/capacity, lead time, MOQ, and order multiple. Where the engine reads a site-specific field, it overrides the material-level default.
  8. Dated scheduled receipts: future supply records stored in mrp.scheduled_receipts (the provided simple CSV inventory upload does not itself load a separate receipt schedule).

8. Exact calculation behavior in the current Node MRP engine

For each selected plant and material, the engine starts from on-hand stock and walks buckets in date order.

8.1 Gross material requirement

For each forecasted finished good and component in its BOM:


component gross demand = finished-good forecast × BOM ratio × (1 + component scrap fraction)

Requirements for the same component/bucket are added together. The engine can recursively create component requirements from a make material's BOM when that make material itself has a planned order.

8.2 Available balance, net need, order, projected stock

Current Node engine logic is:


starting projected stock = on_hand_qty
scheduled supply in bucket = scheduled receipts + dated/undated in-transit allocation
stock before planned order = prior projected stock + scheduled supply − gross requirement
net requirement = max(0, safety minimum − stock before planned order)
planned order = 0 if net requirement is 0; otherwise max(net requirement, MOQ)
projected inventory = stock before planned order + planned order

Important details when interpreting this formula:

  • The calculation follows the training-template rule and does not subtract reserved_qty, despite the Inventory page displaying an available-stock number that does subtract reservations.
  • A zero-dated in_transit_qty amount has no effect. A positive amount with no due date is counted in the first planning bucket. A positive dated amount is assigned to the matching planning bucket if that bucket falls within the horizon.
  • The inventory upload field scheduled_receipts_qty is loaded as near-term scheduled receipts and included in W0 / the first period.
  • The current Node calculation applies MOQ but does not round up to order_multiple and does not cap the order at maximum stock/capacity. The database stores order multiples and several maximum fields, but stored data does not mean a rule is active in this calculation.
  • Reserved quantities and reorder levels are similarly not inputs to this forward calculation. This is a known distinction between operational display/schema fields and the active training-template calculation.
  • Material UOMs are not automatically converted. A BOM ratio in tonnes against a product forecast in kilograms will produce a wrong requirement unless the source data expresses compatible units.

8.3 Lead time and Expedite

Lead time is configured in buckets, not supplier calendar days, in this forward engine. To calculate a release date, the engine shifts from the order's need bucket earlier by its lead-time bucket count. If the resulting release date is on or before the first planning bucket date, the row receives Expedite / an expedite alert. This does not check today's live date, supplier confirmation, or purchase-order status.

8.4 Capacity check

The projected quantity is compared against the selected capacity setting (plant-material max storage capacity, max stock, inventory override, or material default according to available values). If projected quantity is greater, the bucket is marked capacity exceeded. No automatic order reduction or rescheduling is performed to fix it.

8.5 Screenshot example

In the prior screenshot, P_AGR · PK-BAG-45 shows 60,000 on hand, minimum 50,000, W0 projected stock 79,993, and a 30,000 order. If W0 receipts are zero, this implies about 10,007 gross demand:


60,000 − 10,007 + 30,000 = 79,993

Before ordering, stock would be 49,993, which is 7 below the 50,000 minimum. An MOQ of 30,000 makes the planned order 30,000, not 7. Expand the row to see the exact gross requirement, receipts, net requirement, and release date rather than relying on the inferred reconstruction.

9. How to conduct and verify a planning run

Before running

  • Confirm that the approved forecast version, period, and plant scope are known.
  • Verify that all referenced plant, product, and material codes exist and are active.
  • Check BOM coverage and units for all forecasted products.
  • Check opening stock, reserved values, transit amounts/dates, and near-term scheduled receipts.
  • Confirm safety minimum, MOQ, lead-time buckets, and capacity with the business owner.

Run

  1. Open Dashboard.
  2. Select country and plant(s).
  3. Select product type/product and material filters if the run should be restricted.
  4. Select a supported 3-, 6-, or 12-month horizon, Weekly or Monthly bucket, and start date.
  5. Select Run MRP and wait for the response.
  6. Review totals and material matrix. For orders, expedite markers, large quantities, or capacity flags, expand the material row.
  7. Open Material Requirement to review saved result rows and filters.
  8. Record the run scope/date and the forecast/source file version used when sharing results.

What demonstrates that it worked

  • CSV upload produces a success notification with imported row count.
  • Dashboard selectors populate with database countries, active plants, products, and materials.
  • MRP returns buckets and material rows for selected forecast/BOMs.
  • The run is saved in mrp.mrp_runs; result bucket rows are saved in mrp.mrp_results.
  • Material Requirement can read saved rows from the latest run.
  • One hand-check reconciles: gross demand, scheduled receipts, stock before order, minimum, MOQ, projected stock, and release date.

An empty result can be caused by no forecast within the selected date window, no active BOM, product/plant mismatch, or filters excluding the expected records. An upload notification alone does not prove a later run used the intended dates or selected plants.

10. Scenario analysis

The Dashboard's Scenario inputs lets a planner override one selected product's forecast for one bucket and/or one material's starting on-hand quantity. When recalculated:

  • the changed values affect that calculation only;
  • the master forecast and inventory rows are not overwritten;
  • scenario input details are recorded with the MRP run;
  • the resulting run/result rows are still saved for history.

Use a scenario to answer a narrow “what if” question, such as “What if the first-week product forecast is 10% higher?” or “What if actual stock is 5,000 lower?” Return to approved input data for the official baseline run.

13. Security, access, and data handling notes

  • Database access is made server-side through the Render API and DATABASE_URL.
  • Current frontend profile/login visuals are not evidence of an integrated authentication or authorization system. Treat the demo URL as accessible to anyone who can reach it unless hosting access restrictions are separately configured.
  • The Settings page's role/access controls are not connected to API authorization.
  • Use synthetic or approved demo data for external demonstrations. Do not upload confidential company, customer, supplier, or employee data to an unapproved environment.
  • A free demo host/database plan is not a production service-level, backup, disaster-recovery, or uptime commitment. Keep approved source files and database backups according to company policy.

14. Current limitations and business decisions still needed

This section is deliberate: a manager should know the boundary of the current tool before interpreting a number as a commitment.

  1. Surrounding pages are not all live. Demand plan editing, purchase orders, production plans, report scheduling, settings, and most master-data catalog editing are UI demos. They do not form a complete connected ERP workflow.
  2. No automatic procurement. Planned orders are recommendations; there is no supplier PO creation, approval, dispatch, acknowledgement, or receipt posting from the MRP result.
  3. Order multiples are stored but not applied in Node MRP. Confirm whether the management template expects rounding to order multiples, then implement/validate that rule if required.
  4. Maximum is an alert, not a hard order cap. Capacity exceedance is flagged; the engine does not automatically reduce or move orders.
  5. Reserved stock differs by screen. Inventory display subtracts reserved stock for “Available Stock”; the forward formula uses on-hand and follows the workbook note to not subtract reserved quantities. Management should confirm which rule is authoritative for live planning.
  6. Forecast UOM aggregation needs care. The dashboard summary labels forecast total as tons while database products carry units. Avoid summing unlike units until the display is made UOM-aware.
  7. No automatic UOM conversion. BOM ratio, forecast, and inventory quantities must already be expressed in compatible units.
  8. Inventory CSV is one balance row per plant/material. The upload handles on-hand and one in-transit due date per material row plus near-term receipt quantity. It does not upload an arbitrary list of multiple dated receipts; those use the separate scheduled-receipts table/API/database process.
  9. Current country CSV import mapping is narrow. Importer currently maps MAR to Morocco/MAD; additional countries need a controlled code change or admin/data setup.
  10. Weekly demand CSV only. The demand importer rejects monthly upload rows. The calculation engine itself supports weekly and monthly buckets when matching data exists.
  11. Lead time uses bucket units. Confirm how days/weeks/months from source planning files map to lead_time_buckets for the chosen bucket mode.
  12. Supplier master is not the forward lead-time source. Material or plant-material lead-time policy drives the forward calculation.
  13. Demo/sample data is not business-approved by default. Some mappings and values in sample files are explicitly synthetic/inferred. They demonstrate workflow and should not be treated as purchase instructions.
  14. No forecast-accuracy result without actuals. The schema can hold actual demand in an extension, but the portal currently does not provide an end-to-end actuals upload and forecast accuracy analysis workflow.

15. Management review checklist

Before approving a result for business use, the reviewer should be able to answer:

  • Which forecast version and source was used?
  • What country, plants, planning start, horizon, and bucket size were selected?
  • Are product and component units compatible? Are BOM ratios correct and complete?
  • Do opening stock and inbound receipts match the latest trusted snapshot?
  • Should reserved quantities reduce MRP availability under the agreed business policy?
  • Are minimum stock, MOQ, order multiple, lead time, and capacity approved for each site/material?
  • Are large orders explained by gross demand, MOQ, or BOM/UOM setup?
  • Are expedite release dates feasible with suppliers and calendar assumptions?
  • Are there capacity warnings or missing forecast/BOM data?
  • Has procurement separately approved and created any actual order?
  • Is the environment and data appropriate for the intended decision (demo versus operations)?

Gross requirementTotal component demand from forecast and BOM before stock is considered.
Intermediate / make materialA component produced internally that can have its own BOM.
Lead timeNumber of planning buckets between releasing supply and needing it.
MOQMinimum order quantity; the smallest suggested order quantity when replenishment is needed.
Net requirementShortfall needed to restore stock to its minimum after demand and receipts.
On handStarting physical inventory quantity recorded for the planning run.
Order multipleDesired lot-size increment (for example, order in multiples of 100); stored today but not used by the current Node forward calculation.
Planned orderSuggested replenishment quantity generated by MRP. Not yet an approved/sent purchase order.
Projected inventoryCalculated expected end-of-bucket stock after demand, receipts, and planned order.
Receipt / scheduled receiptSupply expected to arrive and increase available material in a future bucket.
Safety stock / minimum stockMinimum balance the plan aims to retain after meeting demand.
ScrapAllowance for material loss during production, entered as a decimal fraction in material master.
UOMUnit of measure, such as each, kg, or tonne.
